The usual way an agency finds out [CPA](/glossary#cpa) has drifted is that the client mentions it first. That's not a knock on any particular team's diligence — it's a natural consequence of checking a single target number against a single day's number, which is a comparison that's almost useless on its own. A campaign's CPA is noisy day to day for reasons that have nothing to do with anything being wrong: day-of-week effects, an auction getting more competitive for a few hours, a single high-value conversion landing at 11:58pm and getting counted in the wrong day's bucket.

## A single target number is the wrong comparison

"Target CPA: $40" tells you almost nothing about whether *today's* $52 is a problem or noise, because it doesn't account for how much a campaign's CPA normally moves day to day. A campaign that swings between $30 and $50 on a normal week and a campaign that's rock-steady at $38–$42 need completely different thresholds for what counts as "off" — but a flat target treats them the same.

## What a rolling baseline catches that a flat target misses

A 7-day rolling view of CPA (or ROAS, or CPC) does two things a flat target can't: it shows you what "normal" actually looks like for *this* campaign, and it makes a real trend — three or four days moving the same direction — visually distinct from a single noisy day. The moment the rolling line crosses a threshold you've set relative to that campaign's own baseline, that's a genuine signal, not a data point that happened to land on the wrong side of a fixed number.

This is exactly what PlanPulse's [anomaly detection](/glossary#anomaly-detection) is built around: a 7-day rolling CPA (and ROAS, and CPC) chart with target thresholds, so the gauge only flags red when there's an actual trend crossing the line — not every time a single day is noisy.

## Catching it early enough to matter

The value of any anomaly alert is entirely a function of how early it fires relative to when the budget is actually spent. An alert that fires after a flight is 90% spent is a postmortem, not a save. That's why PlanPulse surfaces anomaly alerts in the [daily AI briefing](/features#ai-reporting) rather than only in a dashboard someone has to remember to open — the alert reaches the account manager the morning a trend starts forming, while there's still budget left to act on.

## Pair it with the setup checks, not instead of them

Anomaly detection catches performance drifting from its own recent history. It won't catch a campaign that's been [misconfigured from day one](/blog/setup-mistakes-that-break-campaign-pacing) — wrong geotargeting, a broken destination URL, the wrong [budget type](/blog/daily-vs-lifetime-budget-overspend) — because a campaign that's been broken since launch doesn't have a "normal" baseline to drift away from. That's a setup problem, not an anomaly, which is why PlanPulse pairs rolling anomaly detection with the Health Check Agent's setup audits rather than relying on either alone.
